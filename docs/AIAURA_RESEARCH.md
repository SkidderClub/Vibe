# AIAura: aiming research and detection assessment

Reviewed 30 October 2026; implementation notes updated 31 October 2026. This assessment separates published claims, engineering inference and local checks. It does not claim access to Polar's private models or establish an undetectable configuration.

## What the primary sources establish

**Human pointing.** Soukoreff and MacKenzie's review describes the relationship between movement time, movement distance and required accuracy, including endpoint variation and evaluation methodology. Applied here, angular target size and angular distance are useful context; one fixed rotation speed cannot describe all aiming situations. This is an application of pointing research, not a Minecraft-specific experimental result. [Soukoreff and MacKenzie, 2004](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/ijhcs2004.html)

**Smooth movement.** Flash and Hogan studied planar arm movement and a minimum-jerk model, including characteristic velocity profiles. This supports considering velocity and acceleration across a movement. It does not establish that every mouse movement follows one easing curve, nor that adding such a curve makes automation indistinguishable from a person. [Flash and Hogan, 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4020415/)

**Learning can imitate some behavior.** GAN-Aimbots demonstrated a learned aim-assistance proof of concept and evaluated it against protection mechanisms in its own FPS study. That result motivates studying learned rather than purely scripted motion. It is not evidence about this implementation, Minecraft, current Polar or every future classifier. AIAura uses supervised behavior cloning, not that paper's GAN architecture. [Kanervisto, Kinnunen and Hautamäki, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07060)

**Temporal detection remains possible.** BotScreen uses a recurrent anomaly detector on aiming sequences near firing events in CS:GO, including carefully configured aim assistance. Its observation pipeline normalizes event timing and considers target-relative aiming. Consequently, smoothness or a believable marginal distribution alone is insufficient evidence of human behavior. This study is not Polar's implementation and cannot supply Polar thresholds. [Choi, Ko and Cha, USENIX Security 2023](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity23-choi.pdf)

**Polar's public scope.** Polar advertises physics-based movement simulation, behavioral machine learning, relevant-packet cloud processing and continuing updates. Those are vendor descriptions, not independent benchmark results. [Polar's official site](https://polar.top/)

Its documentation lists Auto Clicker, Heuristics, Invalid Protocol, Movement and Tick Speed, among other categories. It describes combat and movement mitigations, delayed cloud punishments and suspicious-clicking/protocol alerts. Therefore, a brief session with no ban cannot establish that no detection or mitigation occurred. These pages do not disclose the classifier weights, complete feature set, training data or internal thresholds. [Polar detections documentation](https://docs.polar.top/detections.html)

Polar also states that checks needing immediate setbacks, such as movement, run locally in the plugin. Treating its entire system as one cloud neural network would be inaccurate. [Polar FAQ](https://docs.polar.top/faq.html)

## Human input and Minecraft's angle representation

The following comes from the local Minecraft 1.8.9 client code and this implementation's tests, rather than claims about a private detector.

The mouse sensitivity conversion is `gain = (sensitivity * 0.6F + 0.2F)^3 * 8F`. Minecraft multiplies integer relative mouse deltas by this float gain, then applies `0.15D` when updating float rotations. Pitch is bounded to ±90 degrees. Arithmetic order and float rounding matter. Computing a nominal angular step and rounding arbitrary rotations afterwards can differ from the actual input path.

AIAura predicts counts, converts to the current sensitivity and applies the native arithmetic. Sub-count fractions are carried between ticks with a bounded remainder. This preserves the representation constraint and is covered by randomized arithmetic tests. It does not prove the resulting count sequence was generated by a physical mouse.

Server angles do not reveal mouse DPI, hand biomechanics or the full OS acceleration path. Relative LWJGL input already reflects the path that reached the client. No synthetic OS acceleration is invented, and no claim is made to identify the user's device from angles. “GCD fix” is not a general humanization method: a quantized automated sequence remains automated.

Useful distinctions include target acquisition versus tracking, reaction delay versus repeat-click timing, proximity to reach, target angular size/motion, overshoot and subsequent corrections, movement-induced camera error, pauses and correlated yaw/pitch changes. Personal demonstrations can contain these effects. A small mean regressor, however, can average away rare or multimodal behavior. Adding sampled angular residuals can introduce visible wobble rather than recover the missing behavior. The updated implementation removes that angular noise, preserves temporal neural context and filters velocity changes. This improves continuity but does not recreate the complete human motor process.

## Detection surface and implemented corrections

This table is an engineering analysis of observable behavior. It does **not** assert that Polar uses every listed measurement.

| Surface | Why it can differ from manual play | Implementation and remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Movement/rotation consistency | Movement simulated with one yaw while another yaw is sent can be internally inconsistent. | Uses KillAura's pre-movement rotation publication and MoveFix input/packet scopes. The recorder never sends movement to a server. Other enabled movement/timer modules can still alter gameplay. |
| Attack packet ordering | A separate packet loop can attack during invalid item-use states or emit actions in a different stage. | Reuses the existing vanilla-input controller and attack-key edges. Native Minecraft creates swing/attack packets. Tests invoke the real click handler and confirm action order before movement. |
| Reach and obstruction | A behavioral model cannot make an out-of-range or blocked hit valid. | Ray intersection uses configured reach and native collision borders. Blocks and intervening entities obstruct attacks. Default reach is 3.0. Increasing it changes reach behavior. |
| Instant acquisition | Reusing one timer for all clicks can erase first-target reaction time. | Records separate aim and first-click reactions, backfills labels on acquisition and restarts deadlines on target changes. The selection heuristic itself is not learned and may still differ from a player's attention. |
| Independent frame jitter | Matching yaw/pitch histograms alone misses temporal and cross-axis structure. | Four-frame neural context, smooth aimpoints, causal velocity filtering, bounded acceleration and stationary-target settling. No sampled angular noise is added. No-target state resets the policy and returns monotonically to the camera. A filter still has a characteristic response and is not evidence of indistinguishability. |
| Click/aim independence | Plausible CPS alone ignores range, hurt state, movement and target changes. | Separate click-intent, interval and hold heads share contextual inputs with aim. Measured click intervals are used only within one target encounter. Correlation beyond the short context is not fully modeled. |
| Perfect center locking | One fixed point can make tracking look unnaturally exact. | Three learned normalized body-point heads, valid crosshair intersection labels and previous-point context. Pitch uses explicit geometric feedback toward that learned point with recorded motor-response limits. This improves vertical tracking but is not evidence that every part of the trajectory was generated by a human. |
| Out-of-view knowledge | Immediate turns to unseen targets can expose information unavailable to the user. | Uses camera FOV, target validity and line-of-sight filters. LockFOV makes the displayed view and target cone follow the spoofed perspective; otherwise the user's camera determines the cone. No target is predicted through walls by default. Peripheral/occlusion visibility remains an approximation. |
| Sensitivity changes | Replaying raw counts at another sensitivity changes all angular speeds. | Transfers demonstrated angular scale into current integer counts using native conversion. Large DPI/device/play-style changes still warrant new demonstrations. |
| Lag catch-up | Replaying every missed deadline after a stall creates concentrated bursts. | Discards stale click backlog and bounds work per input pass. Arena recording pauses on focus loss or long frames. Very high boosts can still produce multiple edges in one game tick. |
| Invalid or absent model | Random weights or partial JSON can produce arbitrary actions. | No production fallback profile. Explicit checkpoint weights, schema dimensions, finite values and timing bounds are checked; invalid files leave AIAura inactive. |
| Data leakage and self-training | Adjacent frames across a random split or recording the model itself can overstate quality. | Chronological validation, a purged boundary and manual-only demonstrations; preview cannot append. Validation guides early stopping/calibration, so it is not an independent test. |
| Limited environment coverage | Arena reactions can differ from real terrain, latency, knockback and target tactics. | Native dry-land movement math and knockback are verified against Minecraft; both sides use the same integrator, collision shapes, hit resistance and reach/occlusion rules. Jumpable terrain, elevated spawns and opponent climbing provide height encounters. A full server, water/ladder simulation, custom knockback and network latency are not modeled. |
| Long-run behavioral identity | Repeated target choice, consistently advantageous timing or a limited repertoire can remain classifiable. | Personal training and measured variation do not eliminate this risk. There is no measured classification result for AIAura. |

The review led to concrete fixes: explicit weight validation; stronger timing/metadata validation; collision-border agreement between arena and live rays; same-target interval labels; required reaction/hold/aimpoint coverage; bounded fractional carry and stale click replay; active-aura lookup for AutoClicker, target visuals and dependent combat modules; and disabling combat during recorder/menu/Freecam conflicts.

## Evaluation and what has not been established

The local checks establish executable properties: backpropagation lowers loss on a test-only learnable chronological fixture; checkpoints reproduce predictions; malformed recordings fail closed; reset cancels publication; boosters and reaction gates follow their settings; preview does not enter the dataset; the actual native click handler emits the expected action sequence; and the recorder draws real frames at several resolutions. Synthetic fixtures are never supplied as a user's personal model.

None of these checks measures a live anticheat's classification. There was no connection to a Polar test server, no access to its cloud decisions and no longitudinal human-versus-AIAura dataset. A model's training/validation loss measures its imitation objective, not its detectability. Gains in attack accuracy can themselves change observed performance and behavior.

A sound future evaluation would freeze versions/settings, use separate manual sessions and unseen encounters, compare unboosted replay with those manual baselines, and measure target-relative trajectories, acquisition delays, click/aim coupling, miss rates, temporal correlations and movement/input consistency. Any assessment of a named anticheat additionally needs controlled server-side observations, including delayed outcomes and mitigations. Until that evidence exists, “bypasses Polar” or “undetectable by AI” would be unsupported claims.
